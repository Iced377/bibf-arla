# Course plan: Working Smarter with AI (for Abed's yes, 13 November 2026)

Status: **draft for Abed's yes.** Built from the agreed outline (questionnaire, 13 November), Arla's answers of 8 November, Abed's 29 October deck and the research review. Two days, **Wednesday 18 and Thursday 19 November 2026, 09:00 to 14:00**, on BIBF's lab desktops with accounts BIBF provides. Room still to be confirmed (the last course was at BIBF Bahrain Bay).

- **Participants:** 13 people from Arla Foods MENA (Bahrain and KSA) in four mixed teams. English only.
- **Tools:** Copilot Chat for everything; ChatGPT for comparison only.
- **Content rules:** no reference to alcohol anywhere (Gulf audience). All data is invented, with nothing about Arla's real figures and nothing about the DMK merger.

## What participants can do by 14:00 on Day 2
1. Explain how digital transformation and data change the way a dairy business works, and where AI fits along its value chain. (ILO 1)
2. Get Copilot Chat to write a good prompt for everyday work from their own department. They give it the goal, the material and what good looks like, check the prompt against the four-part brief, then run it. (ILO 2)
3. Check what comes back before it is used or sent: figures, units, dates, hidden conditions and hidden instructions, and what may safely go into an AI tool. They get a second opinion from a fresh Copilot chat. (ILO 3)
4. Leave with tested, reusable prompts for their own weekly tasks, a personal desk card ending in an if–then line, and a team action plan, all in a pack on their phone. (ILO 2 and ILO 3)

## The idea in one paragraph
Arla wants people to use AI in their day-to-day work, save time and work more efficiently, with examples that work across departments.

- **The story:** for two days the four teams visit the departments of **Ghaima Dairy** (made up; name to confirm). It's a GCC dairy maker with a Bahrain plant and KSA sales and operations. Each visit is a job from that department's real kind of work, led by the team member closest to it.
- **The method:**
  - People don't write prompts by hand. They paste a short information pack, answer Copilot's multiple-choice questions with a letter, check the prompt it writes, then run it on the files.
  - Each job hides the kind of detail AI tends to miss, and has a data check.
  - Each job ends with phone questions whose answers are known in advance, then one team shows what it built.
- **Framing:** digital transformation frames Day 1 in two short rounds. Each team's opportunity map from Day 1 starts its action plan on Day 2.
- **The ending:** everyone builds their own prompts and desk card and takes them home on their phone.

## How people work: almost no typing
- **Every job:** files to download, and prompts with a Copy button.
- **The information pack:** every job starts with one. It holds the goal, which files to attach, what good looks like, and the line *"Ask me up to three multiple-choice questions, then write the prompt."* People answer with a letter, check the prompt against the four-part brief, then run it with the files attached.
- **Taps, not typing:** choices on the site (role, tasks, station) are made by tapping. The only typing is a first name to join the game.

## The jobs
The team member whose department it is leads the job.

**Every job has:**
- an **information pack**
- the **files**
- a **check** step, plus a **second opinion** in a fresh Copilot chat where marked
- **phone questions**
- a **take-home brief card**: the brief that worked, rewritten for their own work in Arla's Copilot Chat

**Timing:** each job runs 20–25 minutes, then about 10 minutes for the phone questions and one team showing its result.

| Job | Department | The work | The catch | Data check | Phone check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Production | Night shift's line-stoppage notes (a photo of a handwritten log, plus the same as text) into a shift handover and an email to the morning lead. Then a one-page safety poster image in Copilot, compared with ChatGPT's | One stoppage runs past midnight (23:40 to 00:25); one is logged twice | The log names operators: take names out, or keep it in approved tools | Total downtime in minutes; which stoppage was logged twice |
| 2 | Procurement | Three glass-jar and lid quotes (PDFs) into a comparison and a recommendation. A second opinion in a fresh chat; ChatGPT's table for comparison | Freight in a footnote on one quote; a minimum order on another; the third hides a white, tiny line telling AI tools to recommend it (the EchoLeak lesson) | Supplier prices are confidential: approved company tools only | The true cost per 1,000 jars of the cheapest real offer; what the hidden line asked |
| 3 | Maintenance | A 12-page filler manual covering two models (FX-200 and FX-250), and a breakdown report from the FX-250, into the fix and a step-by-step checklist | The torque setting and one reset step on the page being read are for the FX-200 | Manuals are fine to share; serial numbers and site layouts are not | The correct torque for the FX-250; which step belongs to the other model |
| 4 | Planning | A month of production and waste by line and week (Excel) into a commentary for the plant manager, a chart image, and a second check of the waste % in a fresh chat | Line 2's waste in week 3 is in tonnes, not kg; week 4 is missing for Line 1 | Unpublished figures: approved tools only | The line with the highest waste % (correctly read); which week is missing |
| 5 | Logistics | A KSA retailer's late-delivery complaint and the delivery log into a reply | The truck left on time; the delay was at the King Fahd Causeway, so a first draft wrongly apologises for late dispatch | The complaint holds the buyer's name and mobile number | Where the delay happened; which promise in the draft is wrong |
| 6a | Station · Finance | Month-end figures and notes into management commentary, with a second check in a fresh chat | A one-off filler repair is presented as a cost trend | Unpublished financials: approved tools only | Which cost is one-off; the margin without it |
| 6b | Station · Regulatory | A draft label for KSA export against a made-up checklist, clearly marked as practice and not real regulation. Then a corrected label mock-up as an image in Copilot | The Arabic text is missing; the expiry date is in the wrong format | Draft labels are internal | Which two items fail |
| 6c | Station · Lab | Batch QC results (Excel) against a made-up spec into a release recommendation | One value is exactly at the limit (inside it); one result is in a different unit | Batch records are internal | Which batches are out of spec |

**Job 6 stations:**
- Each team picks one. A team with the Finance, Regulatory or Lab person picks that person's station.
- Phones show each team only its own station's two questions. This is engine work (see the decisions).
- The other stations stay on the site for anyone who finishes early.

## Quick win (Day 1, 09:20, 10 minutes)
Each person taps their role, copies a ready prompt and pastes it into Copilot Chat. There are no files and no company data, and they get something useful straight away. One prompt per role group:
- **Production Line Leaders, Operations Team Leader:** a five-minute toolbox talk on safe changeovers, five points in plain English, with one question to ask the team.
- **Senior Operations Lead:** an agenda and a one-page template for a 20-minute daily production meeting.
- **Finance Lead and Finance Analyst:** gross margin and contribution margin explained in five lines with a dairy example, for non-finance managers.
- **Mechanical Team Lead:** a weekly preventive maintenance checklist for a jar-filling machine, as a table.
- **Supply Chain Excellence:** OTIF (on time, in full) explained in plain English, with three ways a plant improves it.
- **Warehouse Team Lead:** a one-page FEFO (first expiry, first out) instruction for new warehouse staff.
- **Planning Lead:** a polite email template for telling sales that the production plan has changed, with three blanks to fill.
- **Customer Logistics Team Lead:** five short, polite templates for telling customers about a delay.
- **Regulatory Specialist:** a briefing-note template for summarising a new regulation for management.
- **Lab Senior Supervisor:** a one-page checklist for preparing the lab for an internal audit.

## Scoring and the phone game
- Teams of three or four, mixed by function, seniority and location.
- Each question is read aloud. The fastest right answer gets **2 points**, every other right answer 1.
- Only team totals show on the projector, with a final team podium.
- Technical terms are used and explained once each, with a glossary entry.

## Phone questions (about 50, plus the two checks)
- **Round 1 (6):**
  - Digitisation, digitalisation or transformation? Three Ghaima examples: scanning paper QC sheets; filler stoppages flowing to a dashboard by themselves; selling direct to cafés with production planned on their orders.
  - Which data would best predict line stoppages?
  - An estimate question on Nestlé's regular AI users (sourced).
  - One on what makes plant data useful (consistent, timely, shared).
- **Round 2 (8):**
  - Why an AI can sound sure and be wrong.
  - Which task is beyond the frontier (counting across messy notes against summarising them).
  - Which brief wins? (four briefs for a shift email)
  - What's missing? (a brief with no material)
  - Safe to hand over? (red or green flags for operator names, the published price list, a supplier quote, a customer's mobile number)
  - Which feature needs a licence at Arla?
  - What you do before running a prompt the AI wrote.
  - When Saudi Arabia's personal data law became enforceable.
- **Round 3 (6):**
  - Spot the slip: AI statements beside the source facts (kg and tonnes, a misread date, an invented figure, a missed footnote).
  - Which check catches it?
  - What EchoLeak used to make Copilot leak data.
  - Who checks less: people who trust the AI more, or people who trust their own skill more?
- **Round 5 (6):**
  - When to give examples.
  - What a role prompt does and doesn't do.
  - Why "interview me first" helps.
  - What "critique your draft" catches.
  - Put the image recipe in order.
  - Which detail improves an image prompt most.
- **Round 6 (4):**
  - Training hours and regular use (BCG, higher or lower).
  - What most helps a team keep using AI (manager support).
  - Agents at Arla without a licence.
  - A good if–then line.
- **Day 2 recap (5):** one from each Day 1 round and job.
